Uranus

Uranus (in the Roman tradition Caelus) — the Primordial Starry Sky, the first consort of Gaia, and the paternal principle of the cosmos. In the archaic worldview, Heaven is not an incorporeal void, but a tangible, solid celestial dome resting upon the shoulders of Earth and showering down upon her the vivifying moisture of rains.

The union of Heaven and Earth was unbroken and literal: Uranus blanketed Gaia completely, leaving no open space between them, because of which the primordial world remained stifled in claustrophobic confinement and twilight.

Uranus as Father and the First Tyrant

Uranus entered mythology as the first supreme sovereign of the Universe and its inaugural tyrant. Consumed by dread before the monstrous might and ferocious visage of his own progeny — the Cyclopes and the Hundred-Handed Giants — he forcibly shoved the newborn behemoths back into the dark womb of Earth, physically barring the world from expanding and evolving.

Uranus was not a villain in the conventional sense. His cruelty sprang from a visceral terror of an uncontrollable future. He desperately sought to preserve the primordial, established, and familiar stasis of the world, wherein any transformation or novel creation inexorably disrupted that order. The dread of surrendering dominion and control eclipsed paternal affection, yet halting the evolution of existence proved impossible: it was precisely his frantic bid to obstruct the natural unfolding of the cosmos that precipitated the sovereign's fatal downfall.

Freezing the Cosmos

The earliest offspring of Earth and Heaven appeared to Uranus far too feral and terrifying. The cosmos had yet to assume harmonious proportions, and any emergence of elemental chthonic force seemed a direct threat to Heaven. Assuming the reins of dominion, Uranus attempted to safeguard the universe from relapsing into primeval Chaos, striving to freeze static tranquility into eternity.

After the Fall

Cronus would suffer the very same fate, overthrown by his own son, setting in motion a merciless cycle of struggle for power.

Even in waning from his throne, Uranus brought forth fundamental forces of the cosmos from his spilled blood:

  • Drops of blood that fell upon the Earth:
    • The Erinyes (Furies): ferocious goddesses of blood vengeance for transgressions against kin;
    • The Archaic Giants: serpent-footed warriors in gleaming bronze panoply;
    • The Meliae: ash-tree nymphs, from whose timber mortals would later fashion the first spears.
  • The severed flesh that fell into the sea:
    • Off the shores of the island of Cythera, around the seed of Uranus, foamed a pure white sea froth, out of which arose Aphrodite Urania — the goddess of love and passion, brought forth motherless from the pure celestial essence.
